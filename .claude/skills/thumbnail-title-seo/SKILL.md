# Thumbnail, Title & SEO for Toddler Content

Load this before writing `episodes/<id>/publish.md`.

## Thumbnail
- One character, large, filling most of the frame, with one exaggerated, unmistakable emotion (per `character-design-toddlers`).
- Flat, high-contrast, saturated background — never busy or photographic-detail-heavy; it must read correctly at 120×90px on a phone.
- At most 2–3 words of text overlay, large, high-contrast outline font, sentence-case (not screaming caps) — the audience for the click is often the parent, but consistency with simple in-video text matters for kids who recognize the channel visually.
- Never show a thumbnail that misrepresents the episode's actual content — both a policy risk (see `youtube-kids-compliance`) and it trains the algorithm to mismatch retention.
- Keep a consistent thumbnail template (character position, text placement, border/frame treatment) across episodes so the channel is instantly recognizable as a "shelf" of consistent content — parents and toddlers both pattern-match on this.

## Title
- Front-load the concrete subject and the character name: `"Ember the Fox is Scared of the Dark | Bedtime Story"` beats vague titles.
- Include the format cue parents search for: "Bedtime Story", "Learn Animal Sounds", "Nursery Rhyme", "Potty Training Song" — match actual parent search terms, not invented jargon.
- Avoid ALL CAPS, excessive punctuation, or clickbait exaggeration — erodes the "responsible/trustworthy" positioning this channel needs against the "AI slop" backdrop (see `research/2026-09-toddler-niche.md`).
- Keep to ~60 characters so it doesn't truncate in search/suggested results.

## Description
- First 1–2 lines (visible before "show more") should restate the concrete content and hook, since this is what shows in search snippets.
- Include timestamped chapters for hybrid episodes (one per item/animal/topic) — helps both discovery and parents scrubbing to a favorite segment.
- Always include the AI disclosure in plain language, not just the Studio checkbox: e.g. "This video uses AI-generated animation combined with real footage." Transparency is a trust asset for this channel, not just a compliance box.
- Credit stock footage sources where the license requires attribution; note it even when not strictly required, for the same trust reasons.

## Tags & playlists
- Tag by character name, topic/subject, format cue ("bedtime story", "nursery rhyme", "learn colors"), and channel name — keep tags genuinely descriptive, not stuffed with unrelated high-traffic terms.
- Every episode belongs in at least one playlist grouped by character or by theme (bedtime, animals, emotions, routines) to encourage session watch time — critical for this niche's economics (see GUIDE.md §7).

## Shorts repurposing
- Identify one 30–60s beat per episode (a song hook, the emotional climax, one hybrid item) as a Shorts candidate at packaging time, not as an afterthought — cut vertically (9:16) with the key word/moment front-loaded in the first 2 seconds.
